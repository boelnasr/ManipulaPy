# v1.4 Task 1.5 Trace-Safety Verdict

**Date:** 2026-07-12  
**Decision:** Conditional GO for the backend architecture and Phase 1 Task 2; proceed with
Tasks 11 and 14, subject to the revised gradient contract below. Task 1.5 does not establish
production Torch or JAX support.

## Question

Can the branchless, functional forms of `transform_from_twist` and product-of-exponentials
forward kinematics preserve numerical behavior while producing correct Torch and JAX
derivatives at zero, mid-range, random, and pi-angle joint configurations?

The original question also required a stable derivative of `rotation_logm` at exact pi. That
requirement is not mathematically valid for the principal SO(3) logarithm: at a half turn the
rotation axis has an unavoidable sign/branch ambiguity. Forward kinematics does not call
`rotation_logm`, so the FK and logarithm contracts are evaluated separately.

## Environment

Both spikes ran on CPU under:

- Linux 6.8.0-124-generic, x86_64, glibc 2.35
- Python 3.10.12
- NumPy 2.2.6
- Torch 2.7.1+cu126, CPU tensors, `torch.cuda.is_available() == False`, default dtype set
  to float64
- JAX 0.4.28 and jaxlib 0.4.28, `CpuDevice(id=0)`, with `jax_enable_x64` enabled by the
  spike before constructing arrays

JAX was supplied from an isolated `/tmp/manipulapy-jax-spike` environment because it is not
installed in the project virtual environment. No GPU derivative claim is made.

## Method

The spikes used independent Torch and JAX implementations of the Task 1 branchless formulas:

- functional `stack` and `concatenate` construction, without indexed writes;
- Rodrigues' formula and the SE(3) product-of-exponentials FK loop;
- a three-joint chain containing coordinate-axis screws;
- complete Jacobians of the flattened 4x4 FK result, not the gradient of an ambiguous
  matrix-valued objective;
- float64 central differences with step `1e-6` as the numerical reference;
- zero, all-pi/2, all-pi, and two deterministic random configurations;
- `torch.autograd.functional.jacobian` and `jax.jacfwd`; and
- Torch tracing plus JAX `jit` to exercise graph capture.

The logarithm probe evaluated x- and z-axis rotations at zero, pi/2, pi, a representative
interior angle (`0.731`), and points approaching pi. It checked the rotation vector
`axis * angle`, recovery of the input coordinate-axis rotation, autodiff finiteness, and
central-difference agreement. The raw axis alone is intentionally not treated as a
differentiable quantity at zero because a zero rotation has no unique axis.

## Results

### Forward kinematics

All FK outputs and autodiff Jacobians were finite. The following values are maximum absolute
differences between autodiff and central differences across the full 16-by-3 Jacobian:

| Configuration | Torch | JAX |
|---|---:|---:|
| zero | `4.9760e-11` | `2.7756e-11` |
| pi/2 | `5.2636e-11` | `2.6755e-11` |
| pi | `1.3961e-10` | `1.3961e-10` |
| random 1 | `9.9472e-11` | `1.0294e-10` |
| random 2 | `1.3381e-10` | `1.3509e-10` |

These results pass the spike threshold of `atol=2e-6`, `rtol=2e-5` by a wide margin. Large
reported elementwise relative errors at zero-valued reference entries are not meaningful;
the absolute error and full-Jacobian finiteness are the controlling criteria. Torch's traced
FK matched eager FK exactly for every configuration. JAX evaluated both FK and its Jacobian
under `jit`.

### Rotation logarithm

At pi/2 and the interior angle, both frameworks returned finite rotation-vector derivatives
matching central differences to approximately `2.9e-11` maximum absolute error. Coordinate-axis
pi rotations had zero primal recovery error, confirming the Task 1 fix for x- and z-axis half
turns.

The endpoint and near-endpoint derivative results were:

- At exact pi, autodiff returned NaN or infinity. Centered finite differences cross the
  principal-log branch cut and are not a valid derivative reference.
- At `pi - 1e-5`, the JAX autodiff/finite-difference discrepancy was about `2.59e-1`; closer
  probes were highly step-sensitive. Torch showed the same unstable transition around its
  `pi - 1e-6` branch threshold.
- At zero, the current masked `arccos` formulation returned NaN derivatives even for the
  rotation vector. This is an implementation issue caused by evaluating a singular inactive
  branch, not evidence that FK is unsafe. The axis-angle pair itself is non-unique at zero;
  the matrix logarithm/rotation vector is the contract that should be made locally smooth.

## Limitations

This was a feasibility spike, not a production-backend test:

- `TorchBackend` and `JaxBackend` do not exist yet; they are Tasks 11 and 14.
- `SerialManipulator.forward_kinematics` still constructs `np.eye(4)` and owns NumPy screw and
  home-pose arrays until Task 2. The prototypes therefore used local functional FK loops.
- The test did not exercise the backend registry, dtype/device conversion, public return
  contracts, production kinematics objects, inverse dynamics, or accelerator devices.
- Only two deterministic random FK configurations were sampled per framework. The retained
  Tasks 12 and 15 contract should broaden this with a fixed-seed parameterized set.
- The prototype scripts lived in `/tmp`; the decision record, rather than prototype code, is
  the retained Task 1.5 artifact.

## Revised Gradient Contract

1. FK acceptance compares the complete Jacobian `d vec(T) / d theta`. If a framework API
   requires a scalar objective, use multiple fixed dense weight matrices
   `f_W(theta) = sum(W * T(theta))`; a plain sum can hide errors through cancellation.
2. Exact zero, pi/2, exact pi, and deterministic random joint configurations remain mandatory
   FK cases. Joint angle pi is not a singularity of FK.
3. `MatrixExp3(MatrixLog3(R))` remains a mandatory primal reconstruction test at identity and
   exact coordinate-axis half turns.
4. Autodiff of `MatrixLog3`/the rotation vector is required at smooth interior rotations and
   through the identity using a locally smooth formulation. Do not require a derivative of the
   raw axis at identity.
5. Exact pi is excluded from logarithm-gradient acceptance. Until Tasks 12 and 15 characterize
   a tighter stable domain, use a conservative principal-log exclusion band
   `abs(pi - theta) <= 1e-4`. This is a documented representation boundary, not a backend failure.
6. Every accepted Jacobian must be finite and satisfy float64 central differences with
   `atol=2e-6`, `rtol=2e-5`. Primal backend parity should use `atol=1e-12`, `rtol=1e-10` where
   the NumPy reference is well-conditioned.

## Decision and Replan

**Task 2:** Proceed. Migrate production FK to backend construction and matrix multiplication,
preserving its functional product-of-exponentials form. Add NumPy/CuPy primal parity now. Do not
claim Torch/JAX trace safety from Task 2 alone.

**Task 11:** Proceed with `TorchBackend`. Its acceptance must include the dtype/device-aware
construction used by the spike and must avoid host conversion or scalar extraction on traced paths.

**Task 12:** Retain `tests/test_gradient_contract.py` as the production test, replace prototype
functions with real backend-dispatched `transform_from_twist` and FK, and use full Jacobians. Fix
the zero-angle rotation-vector NaN. Treat exact-pi log behavior as an explicit `xfail` only if a test
documents the mathematical branch boundary; it must not weaken exact-pi FK or primal reconstruction.

**Task 14:** Proceed with `JaxBackend`, requiring functional purity and x64 in derivative tests.
The isolated JAX result proves formula feasibility, not registry integration. Verify `jax.jit` on
the production FK path after backend and kinematics migration.

**Task 15:** Mirror the Task 12 production contract with `jax.jacfwd` or `jax.jacrev`, including
the zero-angle rotation-vector fix and the principal-log exclusion band. Reassess the `1e-4` band
empirically and document any change. Add inverse-dynamics gradients only after Task 3's traced-array
cache bypass is in place.

The overall verdict is therefore **GO for implementation, with a narrowed and mathematically
correct log-gradient promise**. A release-level GO still requires the real Torch and JAX adapters,
production FK integration, retained gradient tests, and Tasks 12 and 15 passing on their supported
CPU/GPU matrix.
